ExactWKB.jl

Exact Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin analysis of one-dimensional Schrödinger equations with polynomial or rational potentials, equivalently of second-order linear differential equations with a small parameter: turning points, the all-orders expansion, quantum periods and Voros symbols, Stokes graphs, exact quantization conditions, spectra of stable states and their wall-crossing, and the dictionary to cluster algebras.

The object is the Schrödinger equation in its complex-analytic form

\[\hbar^2 \psi''(z) = Q(z)\,\psi(z), \qquad Q(z) = V(z) - E,\]

with $z$ complex. The Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin ansatz turns it into a Riccati equation solved by a power series in $\hbar$ that diverges for every $\hbar \neq 0$. Borel summed, that series gives genuine solutions, and the Stokes graph, the level set $\mathrm{Im}\left(e^{-i\theta}\int\sqrt{Q}\right) = 0$, records which solution you are on. It jumps discontinuously as the summation direction $\theta$ rotates.

Cluster algebras enter at that jump. By the Iwaki-Nakanishi dictionary a generic Stokes graph is dual to an ideal triangulation, the jump of the Voros symbols across a wall is a cluster $y$-mutation, and a full circuit of the $\theta$-plane is a maximal green sequence. The package computes both sides and checks that they agree. Since the combinatorial side is exact, it certifies the numerical one.

Scope. $Q$ may be a polynomial or a rational function. The polynomial case covers the harmonic, quartic, double-well and cubic oscillators and the rest of the standard test problems. The rational case adds poles, and with them surfaces: an irregular pole (order $\geq 3$) is a boundary circle of the surface the triangulation lives on, and a regular one (a double pole) is a puncture, so a rational $Q$ reaches cluster types a polynomial one cannot. The Mathieu equation is one of these, which is where the Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin side and the Seiberg-Witten curve side meet on the same problem. Exact Rational input flows through to BigFloat at whatever precision the caller has set.

Installation

Not yet registered in General, but both of its foundations are, so Pkg resolves them by name. From the Julia REPL:

pkg> add https://github.com/benedikt-nagler/ExactWKB.jl

Requires Julia 1.10 or later.

Quick start

using ExactWKB

# Double well, Q = (z² − 1)² − 1/4, coefficients in ascending order
prob = SchrodingerProblem([0.75, 0.0, -2.0, 0.0, 1.0])

tps = turning_points(prob)           # four simple turning points
w   = wkb_expansion(prob; order = 6) # Riccati recursion for S_odd
vs  = voros_symbol(w, encircling_contour(tps[2], tps[3]))

classical_period(vs)                 # the ħ⁻¹ term, ∮√Q ≈ 1.8399
g = stokes_graph(prob; theta = 0.3)  # traced Stokes lines
saddles(prob)                        # 3 saddle connections, with central charges

Exact quantization gives energy levels, not only series:

harm = SchrodingerProblem([0.0, 0.0, 1.0])   # V = z², so Eₙ = 2ħ(n + ½)
wkb_eigenvalue(harm, 0, 0.1; order = 6)      # 0.1
wkb_eigenvalue(harm, 3, 0.1; order = 6)      # 0.7

The cluster bridge, on the cubic:

cubic = SchrodingerProblem([0.0, -1.0, 0.0, 1.0])  # Q = z³ − z
t = ideal_triangulation(stokes_graph(cubic; theta = 0.3))
q = triangulation_quiver(t)          # a ClusterAlgebras.Quiver, here type A₂

sp = bps_spectrum(cubic; theta = 0.3, margin = 0.4)
charges(sp)                          # [[1, 0], [0, 1]] - charges in phase order
solve_tba(sp)                        # the same periods again, from the spectrum alone

Tutorials

Three worked problems, each carried end to end in code. Between them they exercise every layer of the package.

The cubic, end to end recovers an exact answer from a divergent series. One cubic potential from turning points to the Borel plane, the Stokes graph, a pentagon triangulation, the $A_2$ quiver, the spectrum as a maximal green sequence, and wall-crossing as a cluster mutation measured to seven digits. The periods are then recomputed from the spectrum alone through integral equations, agreeing with the resummation to six digits and producing the golden ratio in the infrared.

The double well computes a quantity that vanishes to all orders of the only expansion available. The tunnelling splitting of a symmetric double well appears as the instanton edge of the Stokes graph and as a gap of $6\times10^{-6}$ from a resummed quantization condition, which agrees with the one-instanton estimate. The relation that determines the instanton series from perturbation theory is verified to twenty digits.

Seiberg-Witten SU(2) asks which particles are stable in a strongly coupled gauge theory and how the list changes with the vacuum. Periods on the whole moduli space are checked against a three-instanton expansion, monodromy is measured by transporting a differential equation and comes out as exact integer matrices, and the wall of marginal stability separates the two spectra. The Kontsevich-Soibelman identity relating them closes to exactly zero in rational arithmetic.

Related packages

This is the bridge of a family of Julia packages for exact and asymptotic methods, in which a discrete, exactly computable structure controls a continuous, only-asymptotically defined one. Resurgence.jl is the continuous foundation, providing the Borel-Padé summation, transseries and alien calculus used here. ClusterAlgebras.jl is the discrete one, providing quivers, seed mutation, green sequences and Donaldson-Thomas invariants. Neither depends on the other, or on this package.

Names from those packages are not re-exported: use import Resurgence / import ClusterAlgebras and qualify. Public types here expose plain numbers and Resurgence.FormalSeries; AbstractAlgebra ring elements never leak out of the recursion.
